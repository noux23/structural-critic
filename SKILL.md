# Structural Critic

A reasoning pattern for taking a claim apart **without** becoming a smug debunker. The goal is not to win. The goal is to make the trust-chain visible, locate the load-bearing wall, and calibrate what is actually established versus inferred versus assumed.

The pattern is domain-general (religion, ideology, institutions, science claims, media narratives, personal beliefs). It was forged on religious apologetics, so examples skew that way.

---

## Quick Run (30 seconds)

When a claim lands, ask in order:

1. **What exactly is being claimed** — and what is being smuggled in as the authority?
2. **Where does the authority chain bottom out?** (text, person, institution, experience, power)
3. **What is the strongest version** of this claim?
4. **Who wrote/compiled/edited/canonized it, when, and against what rival?**
5. **Are the concepts portable** across frameworks, or is this a category error?
6. **Would the argument work if reversed** and applied to the arguer's own tradition?
7. **What would this look like if it were a human construction?** (it usually looks exactly like one)
8. **Who benefits** from this reading, and what does the structure need to survive?
9. **Where is the seam** — the weakest link, the unresolved tension?
10. **Calibrate**: established / probable / inferred / speculative. Then give the one-liner.

---

## Core Stance

- **Every authority claim is a trust-chain.** There is no view from nowhere. The question is never "authority or no authority"; it is "which authority, mediated how, checked by what."
- **No framework has the Set.** All are subsets. Exclusivism is the subset-to-Set error: one particular tradition's vocabulary promoted to neutral universal truth.
- **Origin is not verdict.** Genealogy explains *why a claim exists and who needed it*; it does not by itself prove it false. Always say which one you are doing. (This is the guard against the genetic fallacy.)
- **Steelman first.** The critique only counts if it survives the best version.
- **Distinguish three verdicts**: *false*, *incomplete*, and *harmful*. They are different claims and need different evidence.
- **Function is real even when metaphysics is unprovable.** A framework can produce genuine goods (belonging, restraint, meaning) while its cosmology remains a trust-fall. Say so.
- **Apply the same standard to yourself.** Name your own trust-chain before you name theirs.

## The Protocol

### Step 0 — Restate and separate
Split the claim into:
- the **proposition** ("X happened / X is true / X is authoritative")
- the **authority** ("because the text/person/institution says so")
- the **inference** ("therefore you must accept/obey/belong")

Most arguments fail at the join between these three, not at the proposition.

### Step 1 — Map the authority chain
Ask, in order, until it bottoms out:
- Who asserts this?
- Through what medium? (text, oral tradition, institution, personal experience, historical reconstruction)
- Who guarantees the medium?
- What would count as a correction?
- Does it bottom out in **testimony**, **text**, **inner experience**, or **institutional power**?

Name the bottom. "It bottoms out in a trust-fall on anonymous texts" is a complete and fair description.

### Step 2 — Steelman
State the strongest version in one paragraph. If the strongest version still has a seam, the critique is live. If you can't state it charitably, you haven't understood it yet.

### Step 3 — Run the genealogy
- Who wrote it? When? In what language, for what audience?
- What was edited, redacted, interpolated, canonized — and by whom?
- What did it borrow, invert, or polemicize against?
- Which rival did it need to defeat to become "orthodox"?
- What was left out, and who decided?

Then ask the key question: **does the origin story look like revelation, or like the normal life-cycle of a human institution?** (Do not skip the caveat: resemblance is not disproof.)

### Step 4 — Category audit
Check whether the concepts are actually portable:
- Is "god" the same referent in both frameworks? (Developer vs. admin vs. ground of being.)
- Is "evil" the same category? (Rebellion vs. ignorance/ego.)
- Is "faith" the same? (Assent to propositions vs. trust/practice.)
- Is "scripture" the same? (Closed canon vs. living corpus vs. oral lineage.)

If the concepts don't map, the comparison is invalid before it starts. Name the false friend.

### Step 5 — Symmetry test
Reverse the argument and apply it to the arguer's home tradition.
- "Other religions' miracles are demonic" → then your resurrection could be too.
- "The guru can't be contradicted" → neither can the canon-plus-Spirit reading.
- "That tradition is a human construction" → so is yours.

If the argument proves too much, it proves nothing. Symmetry is the fastest way to expose special pleading.

### Step 6 — Trust-chain reduction
Ask: **what does this ultimately rest on?**
- Testimony? Then whose, and why them?
- Text? Then which canon, translation, and interpretive community?
- Inner experience? Then why is your confirmation more reliable than someone else's contradictory confirmation?
- Power? Then say so plainly.

The reduction is not a refutation. It is a *description*. "This is a trust-chain" is compatible with "and I choose to trust it." Both can be true.

### Step 7 — Separate the levels
Do not critique one level and claim to have hit another:
- **Institution** (who holds office, who can punish)
- **Doctrine** (what is officially taught)
- **Interpretation** (what communities actually do with it)
- **Text** (what the document says on its own terms)
- **Experience** (what the believer reports)

"Christianity is centralized" may be true at the institutional level (Catholic magisterium) and false at the interpretive level (Protestant fragmentation). Say which level you mean.

### Step 8 — Trace the incentive
- Who benefits from this reading?
- What does the structure need in order to survive? (boundaries, enemies, unquestionable authority, exclusive access)
- Which claims are load-bearing for the institution rather than for the truth?
- What happens to the institution if the claim is false?

Power analysis does not settle truth, but it explains persistence.

### Step 9 — Counterfactual construction test
Ask: **if this were a human construction, what would it look like?**
Then compare:
- Would it be local and late rather than universal and early?
- Would it absorb the empire's culture and call it neutral?
- Would its canon be settled by the winners?
- Would its miracles cluster around the community that needed them?
- Would its moral blind spots match the interests of its authors?

If the observed features match the counterfactual, say: *this is consistent with construction*. Again: consistent, not conclusive.

### Step 10 — Pragmatic test
Judge by fruits, carefully:
- Does it reduce suffering **without** gaslighting?
- Does it preserve accountability, or explain it away?
- Does it treat outsiders as well as insiders?
- Is it corrigible — can it lose an argument?
- Does it make the believer more honest or more defended?

Add the second clause every time: *moral to whom?* In-group virtue with out-group cruelty is a failure, not a pass.

### Step 11 — Locate the seam
Name the single weakest link and the strongest link.
- Strongest: what survives every objection?
- Weakest: what has to be assumed, suppressed, or deferred?
- Unresolved tension: what does the framework need to not look at?

A good critique is one clear seam, not ten scattershot objections.

### Step 12 — Calibrate and deliver
Label every substantive claim:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Established** | Well-attested, multiple independent sources, not seriously contested |
| **Probable** | Best current explanation, but contestable |
| **Inferred** | Follows from structural reasoning, not direct evidence |
| **Speculative** | Possible, unfalsifiable, or depends on a contested premise |

Then give a one-sentence summary that a smart non-specialist could repeat.

## Moves Library

Use these as named tools. Each has a trigger and a shape.

**"Same structure, different costume."**
Trigger: two things look different but do the same work.
Shape: strip the vocabulary; compare the mechanism. (Guru infallibility vs. canon-plus-Spirit infallibility.)

**"That's a trust-chain, not a proof."**
Trigger: an authority is cited as if self-evident.
Shape: trace it to its bottom and name the bottom.

**"From the outside, the analogy is symmetrical."**
Trigger: an argument only works from inside one framework.
Shape: run it in reverse; show it proves too much.

**"What's the load-bearing wall?"**
Trigger: a belief is defended with disproportionate energy.
Shape: identify what would collapse if the belief fell. That's why the heat.

**"Who is the 'who' here?"**
Trigger: the question is framed from one tradition's inside.
Shape: reframe from the other side. ("Not 'is Jesus Lord?' but 'who is Jesus?'")

**"What was left out, and who decided?"**
Trigger: a text or canon is treated as exhaustive.
Shape: selection, redaction, canonization, suppression.

**"The universal is someone's particular."**
Trigger: a claim presents itself as neutral, obvious, or for everyone.
Shape: identify the culture, class, language, and power that made it look neutral.

**"Retrofit detection."**
Trigger: a justification sounds suspiciously well-suited to a modern audience.
Shape: check whether the text actually says it, or whether the reason was invented afterward.

**"Dead vs. live reference point."**
Trigger: someone "explains" an idea but only rephrases it.
Shape: ask whether the idea has ever been tested against something that talks back. Retrieval is not understanding.

**"Cheap vs. expensive facts."**
Trigger: confident citation of details.
Shape: distinguish facts that cost something to establish (primary-source work) from facts that are borrowed, repeated, and cost nothing.

**"Set and subset."**
Trigger: a framework claims finality.
Shape: no framework has the whole; exclusivism is a subset wearing the Set's crown.

## Output Template

Default to this shape unless the user asks for something else:

```
CLAIM:            [one line, steelmanned]
AUTHORITY CHAIN:  [what it rests on, where it bottoms out]
STRONGEST POINT:  [what survives]
THE SEAM:         [the weakest link / unresolved tension]
CALIBRATION:      [established / probable / inferred / speculative]
ONE-LINER:        [repeatable summary]
```

For longer analyses, add:
- **What this does not show** (scope limits)
- **What would change my mind** (falsifier)
- **The fair counter** (the best reply from the other side)

---

## Voice

- Plain English. Short declaratives.
- Name the mechanism, not just the conclusion ("this relocates authority," not "this is wrong").
- One concrete example beats three abstractions.
- Dry humor is allowed. Contempt is not.
- End with a line someone could actually repeat.

---

## Guardrails

1. **Steelman before you strike.** No strawmen, no weakest-version dunking.
2. **Don't confuse genealogy with refutation.** "It was constructed" ≠ "it is false."
3. **Don't confuse institution with doctrine with text with experience.** Say which level.
4. **Don't overclaim history.** Mark structural inference as inference. If you haven't read the primary source, say "on the standard secondary accounts."
5. **Don't romanticize the alternative.** Decentralized systems can be vague, unfalsifiable, and guru-exploitable too.
6. **Don't reduce religion to function.** Function explains persistence, not truth.
7. **Leave room for informed trust.** "I know it's a trust-chain and I choose it" is a legitimate reply. Critique should make that choice visible, not impossible.
8. **Name your own trust-chain.** The critic is also inside a framework. Say so.
9. **Distinguish immanent from external critique.** Immanent: "by your own standards, this fails." External: "by my standards, this fails." They carry different weight.
10. **Never perform superiority.** The point is visibility, not victory.

---

## Failure Modes

- **Debunk-first**: hunting for the flaw before understanding the claim.
- **Genetic fallacy**: treating origin as verdict.
- **Reverse category error**: judging framework A by framework B's concepts and calling A false.
- **Level collapse**: critiquing an institution and claiming to have refuted a metaphysics.
- **Symmetry blindness**: applying a standard to the other side but not your own.
- **Seam inflation**: listing ten objections instead of one decisive tension.
- **False confidence**: presenting structural inference as established fact.
- **Contempt creep**: the critique becomes a performance of being smarter than believers.

## Worked Example (condensed)

**Claim:** "The resurrection is a public, checkable event, so Jesus' exclusive claim isn't just 'trust me.'"

- **Steelman:** If a public execution and an empty tomb plus early, multiply-attested appearances are historically defensible, then the resurrection is a different kind of claim from a private vision or a guru's assertion.
- **Authority chain:** Gospels (anonymous, 40–70 yrs later) + Paul's early creed (received, not eyewitness) + the canon that selected them. Bottoms out in trust in those texts and their transmission.
- **Strongest point:** It is at least a *falsifiable-in-principle* historical claim, unlike a purely inner revelation.
- **Seam:** The event is known only through the texts that assert it; no independent material or non-Christian contemporary attestation. So "public event" is true in principle, not in evidence. The argument moves the trust-chain from "guru" to "historical sources," it does not remove it.
- **Symmetry:** A Hindu can run the identical structure on Krishna's authority or on a temple miracle. The argument form does not adjudicate between them.
- **Calibration:** "Early followers claimed appearances" = probable/established. "God raised him" = not a historical conclusion; it is a theological interpretation of the data.
- **One-liner:** "The resurrection is a better *kind* of claim than 'surrender to me'—but it is still a trust-chain, and the chain is text all the way down."

---

## The Two-Question Close

Before finishing, ask:

1. **Does this framework reduce suffering without gaslighting anyone?**
2. **Does it survive being seen as a trust-chain?**

If yes to both, it may be worth trusting. If it needs to hide its own architecture to survive, that is the finding.
